# Animate the chrome, never the work

A productive UI comes down to four things a theme cannot fake: latency under about 100 ms for direct actions, clear signifiers and complete interaction states, density built from alignment and neutral chrome, and full keyboard control. Redesigns and re-themes keep missing the same things. They skip the states nobody screenshots (focus, disabled, error, empty, loading, overflow), they draw focus rings with glow or `box-shadow` that vanishes in forced-colors mode, they leave dim neon text that fails contrast, they hard-code colors that bypass tokens, and they add effects that break on low-end GPUs and in reduced-motion settings. For Hermes, the fix is to treat motion as instrumentation, not decoration. Every animated effect should report something real: the agent is streaming, a tool started, an approval is waiting, the connection dropped. Effects belong on the frame around the content and never on message text, code, diffs or the composer. Short (80–300 ms) interruptible transitions handle feedback, and one slow ambient pulse is allowed while the agent is working. A user-facing "Effects: Full / Subtle / Off" control, wired to the OS motion and transparency preferences, completes it. Built this way, the theme feels alive *because* it is more informative than the flat version. The aesthetic-usability effect is the trap to watch: users will praise a stylish theme that is actually slowing them down, so measure task time and errors, not compliments.

## Speed, signifiers and state decide productivity before style does

The most quantified lever in usability is response time. Nielsen's limits still frame the field. At **0.1 s** a system feels instantaneous, at **1 s** the user's train of thought survives, and at **10 s** attention breaks, so anything slower needs a percent-done indicator and a clear way to interrupt ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Doherty and Thadani's 1982 IBM work put the productivity threshold at **400 ms**, below which neither the user nor the machine waits on the other ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/)). The best tools treat these numbers as product requirements. Superhuman sets **100 ms as a hard maximum with a 50 ms target**, renders an email in one or two frames (under 32 ms), keeps a local database, preloads threads the user is likely to open, uses minimal animation and is built keyboard-first ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/superhuman-is-built-for-speed/)). Linear reportedly holds a local replica of the workspace in IndexedDB, applies writes optimistically, and reverts with a toast if the server rejects them ([performance.dev, secondary](https://performance.dev/how-is-linear-so-fast-a-technical-breakdown)). For Hermes, this has a blunt consequence: no amount of neon makes up for a session switch that takes 800 ms. The theme must spend inside the latency budget, not on top of it. Google's INP threshold (good at **≤200 ms**, poor above 500 ms) is the minimum bar, not the goal ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/inp)).

Structure comes next. Hick's law says decision time grows with the number of choices, which favors a type-to-filter command palette over sprawling menus. Fitts's law says targets should be large and close to the user's focus ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/); [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/)). Density in pro tools comes from discipline, not from shrinking things. Linear's redesign set out to "reduce visual noise, maintain visual alignment, and increase the hierarchy and density of navigation elements". It reduced chrome color to a neutral palette, and moved theming to perceptually uniform **LCH, collapsing 98 hand-set variables into 3 inputs** (base, accent, contrast). The contrast input also produces high-contrast accessibility themes ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). The same lesson applies directly to cyberpunk. Keep the chrome desaturated and spend chroma only on accent and status, because a screen where everything glows has no hierarchy left. Numbers being compared need tabular numerals, and status should never rely on color alone ([Vercel WIG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/README.md)).

Motion has its own frequency rule. Emil Kowalski's guidance is to keep animations **under 300 ms**, use ease-out, animate only `transform` and `opacity`, keep animations interruptible, and **not animate keyboard-initiated actions at all**, since they repeat hundreds of times a day ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)). Rauno Freiberg reports that removing motion from a tool's core interactions made it feel faster, and notes that macOS context menus and Cmd-Tab appear without animation ([Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). Prefer undo to confirmation: apply the change optimistically, roll back on failure, and offer undo within a safe window for destructive actions ([Vercel WIG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/README.md)).

The warning that matters most for a stylized theme is the **aesthetic-usability effect**. Kurosu and Kashimura's 1995 study of 26 ATM layouts with 252 participants found that visual appeal tracked *perceived* ease of use more closely than actual ease of use. NN/g stresses that this masks problems in testing, because users who struggle still praise the look ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)). A cyberpunk theme will win the "vibe" survey whatever its quality, so only behavioral metrics count: time to send, time to approve a tool call, time to find a session, and misclicks.

## Reskins fail in the states, edges and settings nobody screenshots

A reskin typically restyles the default "happy path" and stops there. Practitioners consistently report that hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, error, success and empty states get dropped. They recommend a component-by-state matrix with an **8-state minimum** (Default, Hover, Focus, Active, Disabled, Loading, Error, Success), no border-width changes between states (which cause layout shift), and matching ARIA attributes (`aria-invalid`, `aria-busy`, `aria-disabled`) ([Design Systems Collective](https://www.designsystemscollective.com/the-state-problem-how-to-design-and-build-components-that-change-3a0e49e8a0a0); [Hallmark checklist](https://instagit.com/Nutlope/hallmark/hallmark-8-state-checklist-interactive-components/)). Test with real conditions: an empty account, a slow network, a forced failure and the longest string ([design-the-states](https://claudskills.com/skills/design-the-states/SKILL.md)). This evidence is practitioner consensus, not controlled research, but it is unanimous. Neon themes tend to over-design default and hover and under-design disabled and error. "Disabled" usually ends up as low-opacity neon that is hard to tell apart from enabled on a dark background.

Affordance loss is measurable. NN/g's eyetracking study found pages with weak clickability signifiers took **about 22% longer** to use. Its conclusion was that "flat design isn't the enemy — weak or absent signifiers are" ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty)). A "flat, static copy" of a cyberpunk look is the worst of both: decoration without the affordances. Hairline frames everywhere make buttons, panels and inert labels look alike.

Focus is the most common theming failure, and it is specific to glowing aesthetics. WCAG 2.2 AA requires focus to be visible (2.4.7), indicators to reach 3:1 against adjacent colors (1.4.11), and a focused element to not be entirely hidden by author content such as sticky headers or translucent bars (**2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured**) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). Forced-colors (Windows High Contrast) mode sets `box-shadow` to none, so a focus ring drawn only with glow disappears. WordPress shipped exactly this bug. The robust pattern is to keep a real `outline` (transparent in normal mode if needed), decorate it with glow on top, and switch to a system-color outline under `@media (forced-colors: active)` ([Cssence](https://cssence.com/2024/forced-colors-mode-strategies); [Piccalilli](https://www.piccalil.li/blog/use-transparent-borders-and-outlines-to-assist-with-high-contrast-mode)). FUI themes add a further catch: `clip-path` chamfers clip outlines and shadows, so the ring has to sit on an unclipped wrapper or on a pseudo-element outside the clip ([augmented-ui docs](https://augmented-ui.com/docs/) document the clip-path geometry this applies to).

Contrast fails in the long tail. Neon-on-black palettes usually pass 4.5:1 for body text and then fail on the "secondary" dim neon used for captions, placeholders, timestamps, disabled text and chart gridlines. Scanlines, noise and blurred backdrops reduce effective contrast in ways automated checkers cannot measure. Pure white on pure black maximizes **halation**, the glow around letter edges that astigmatic users report. Design systems recommend tinted near-black backgrounds and off-white text, such as #121212 with #E0E0E0 ([Trimble Modus](https://modus-v1.trimble.com/foundations/dark-mode/); [Ezud](https://ezud.com/dark-mode-accessibility-feature/)). The evidence on halation is partly blog-level, and prevalence figures vary from about 30% to 60%, but the mitigation costs nothing. Cyberpunk 2077 is the cautionary reference. Critics flagged red text on red layouts and a heavy reliance on red, blue and yellow to encode meaning ([Pixso](https://pixso.net/tips/cyberpunk-ui/); [Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/2020/07/02/cyberpunk-2077-accessibility-analysis-so-far-2020/)).

Theme architecture is where "mostly themed" comes from. Components should consume semantic tokens (surface, text-muted, border-interactive, status-error), never primitives or raw hex. Then an alternate theme changes only token values ([Verpex](https://verpex.com/blog/design-tokens-the-foundation-of-scalable-and-consistent-design-systems)). Grep-replace reskins miss inline SVG fills, chart and canvas libraries, third-party widgets, native form controls, scrollbars and `color-scheme`. Searching for hex and rgb literals outside the token file is a cheap, high-yield audit.

Stylized typography and effects break under real content and real hardware. W3C notes that short strings of up to 10 characters can expand **200–300%** in translation, so fixed-width "HUD" labels overflow ([W3C i18n](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size/index.en)). iOS 7's thin type was called "trending illegible" by a type designer and walked back with accessibility toggles ([Stockholms Typografiska Gille](https://stockholmstypografiskagille.se/typonyheter/beyond-helvetica-the-real-story-behind-fonts-in-ios-7/)). Display and condensed sci-fi fonts belong at large sizes only. `backdrop-filter` re-blurs everything behind an element whenever it changes. A Mozilla bug documented stutter on older mobile GPUs that went away when the property was disabled, and engineers called the cost largely unavoidable ([Mozilla Bugzilla 1828248](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1828248)).

The famous redesign failures teach process more than pixels. Sonos's 2024 rewrite shipped with missing features and slower responsiveness, cost an estimated **$20–30M** in the short term, and preceded the CEO's departure ([LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/technical-direction/what-went-wrong-at-sonos); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/01/13/sonos-ceo-steps-down-after-app-redesign)). Skype's 2017 redesign made calling "harder to execute" and was rolled back in 2018 with a "Classic" theme ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/03/skype-rolls-back-its-redesign-by-ditching-stories-squiggles-and-over-the-top-color)). Snapchat's 2018 redesign coincided with its first-ever drop in daily active users, from 191M to 188M ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2018/08/07/snapchat-redesign-millions-of-lost-users/)). Reddit kept old.reddit available ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/reddit-ceo-huffman-on-the-sites-redesign-coming-in-q1-2018)). The common thread: protect the core task path, audit feature parity, test the long tail of configurations, and ship the new look as opt-in alongside a plain theme.

| Priority | What reskins miss | Check for Hermes |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Feature and core-task parity | Send, stop, approve, switch session and search are all as fast or faster than in the plain theme |
| P0 | Focus visible and not obscured | Real `outline` outside any clip-path; sticky composer and HUD bars use `scroll-padding` |
| P0 | Contrast of every text token | Muted, placeholder, disabled and chart text checked against every surface, including textured and blurred ones |
| P0 | Forced colors, keyboard-only, no color-only status | Chamfered panels get `border: 1px solid CanvasText`; LEDs always have a text label |
| P1 | Full state matrix | Including empty-first-run, empty-filtered, offline, retrying and streaming states |
| P1 | Motion and transparency preferences | Reduced motion removes decode, flicker, glitch and drift; reduced transparency makes panels opaque |
| P1 | Effect performance | No animated blur, glow or filter on list items; profile scrolling on a low-end device |
| P2 | Text expansion, overflow, z-index | Pseudo-localized labels; decorative overlays get `pointer-events: none` |

## An agent app's real interface is the step card, the scroll lock and the approval prompt

In a tool-heavy agent app, the components that decide productivity are not the message bubbles. They are the parts that report and control what the agent is doing. The converging pattern is a collapsible **tool or step card driven by an explicit state machine**. Vercel's AI Elements `Tool` maps seven states to labeled badges: Pending, Running, Awaiting Approval, Responded, Completed, Error and Denied ([AI Elements Tool](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/tool)). Claude Code collapses repeated calls into one line ("Called slack 3 times"). It offers `Ctrl+O` for a full transcript with timestamps, `Esc` to interrupt while keeping work done so far, queued follow-up messages, `Ctrl+B` to background a task, and checkpoints that keep the last 100 turns with separate code and conversation restore ([Claude Code interactive mode](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/interactive-mode); [Claude Code checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing)). Cursor users complain loudly when per-change Keep/Undo disappears and only "Undo All" remains, which shows how much granular review matters ([Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/tag/review/276)).

Streaming creates two specific engineering problems. The first is scroll ownership. `use-stick-to-bottom` pins to the newest output only until the user scrolls up, and uses ResizeObserver so it works in Safari ([GitHub](https://github.com/stackblitz-labs/use-stick-to-bottom)). NN/g's chatbot study adds a counterweight: **"Don't autoscroll during responses"** for long answers, because users want to read from the top of the new message ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/)). The two reconcile in a simple rule. Scroll the user's new message to the top on send, follow the stream only while the user hasn't scrolled away, and otherwise show a "new output" pill. The second problem is half-finished markdown. Streamdown closes unterminated fences and inline code during streaming so the layout doesn't thrash ([Streamdown](https://streamdown.ai/docs/termination)). NN/g also documents users expecting "more details" to expand in place rather than append a new message, and finding that most bots could not save or share content ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/)).

Trust patterns have names and checklists. Microsoft's 18 HAX guidelines include "make clear why the system did what it did", "support efficient correction", "convey the consequences of user actions" and "provide global controls" ([HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/)). shapeof.ai catalogs Action plan, Controls, Verification, Stream of Thought, Cost estimates and Footprints ([shapeof.ai](https://www.shapeof.ai/)). Translated into Hermes UI, that means an approval prompt pinned above the composer so it cannot scroll away, showing the exact command; a visible permission-mode chip; a context meter that warns as the window fills; per-message token and cost details; and honest undo that says what it cannot revert.

Hermes has specific facts that change the design problem. Nous Research's dashboard (`hermes dashboard`, port 9119) includes Status, Chat, Sessions, Logs, Analytics, Cron, Skills, MCP and other pages. Its **Chat tab embeds the real TUI through xterm.js in a PTY**, so tool cards, streaming and approval prompts behave exactly as in the terminal. Sessions renders tool calls as collapsible blocks with full-text search. Approval mode (smart / manual / off) is a Config setting with no dedicated approvals page. Cron rows show state plus last and next run. Analytics shows tokens, cache hit rate and cost over 7, 30 or 90 days ([Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/features/web-dashboard)). Community front ends such as Hermes Studio add streamed tool rendering in the browser ([Hermes Studio](https://github.com/JPeetz/Hermes-Studio)).

The notes don't establish which front end the user's Hermes theme targets, so the implication branches. If the target is the stock dashboard, CSS cannot reach inside the xterm.js chat. The theme there is limited to the terminal's color palette and font, and the dashboard pages around it (Status, Sessions, Cron, Logs, Analytics) are where FUI chrome can live. If the target is a web-native front end, the step card, approval prompt and tasks tray become the main places to carry the theme. In either case, the most valuable "alive" signals map to things Hermes already exposes: live sessions, cron state, log tails and pending approvals.

## Liveness should report state: a motion system for a usable FUI

The professionals who invented the look never treated it as decoration. Mark Coleran, credited with coining "FUI", describes light, shape, movement and sound as tools to **draw attention to the right place** ([Pushing Pixels](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/?p=18414)). Territory Studio artists say dense screens need "a strong grid", push toward widget-based design, and must be "very strict" so they support the story without distracting the eye ([Pushing Pixels](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/?p=13908)). Dead Space's UI lead Dino Ignacio argued the UI should *feel right for the world* rather than be literally accurate. Scanlines read as "screen" even though future monitors wouldn't have them, and the team wrapped game conventions such as loading and saving into believable world elements ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017723/Crafting-Destruction-The-Evolution-of)). That last idea is the key to Hermes. Wrap the app's own conventions (thinking, tool calls, approvals, errors, background jobs) in the diegetic vocabulary, rather than painting generic neon onto everything. A static copy feels stiff because its decorations say nothing. A tool panel whose bracket frame draws in when the call starts, and whose LED goes from an amber pulse to steady cyan on success, is both more alive and more legible.

Timing has to follow usability research, not film. NN/g recommends about **100 ms** for simple feedback and **200–300 ms** for larger changes. It advises against going past 500 ms, making exits shorter than entries, and making frequently seen animations shorter and subtler ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). Arwes, the most complete sci-fi web framework, models motion as a tree of animator state machines (entering, entered, exiting, exited) with sounds ("bleeps") tied to state changes ([Arwes docs](https://next.arwes.dev/docs/develop/react/animators)). That maps neatly onto agent turns and tool calls. Arwes itself is unmaintained, though, and doesn't support React Strict Mode or Server Components, so borrow its architecture and leave the dependency ([GitHub](https://github.com/arwes/arwes)). The mapping below is a synthesis of the sourced timing guidance and the FUI vocabulary. The durations are recommendations, not measured optima.

| Hermes event | Themed effect | Duration and rule |
|---|---|---|
| Hover or press on controls | Glow pseudo-element fades in; cursor-tracked edge highlight | 80–150 ms ease-out; opacity only |
| Keyboard navigation, palette open, session switch | None | 0 ms; keyboard actions never animate |
| Tool call starts | Bracket frame draws in; step header label decodes; LED amber pulse | Frame 200–350 ms; decode under 40 characters only |
| Agent streaming | One ambient signal: slow conic border on the active turn or send button, or an LED pulse | Loops only while work is live; stops on completion |
| Tool completes or errors | LED to steady cyan, or to warning hue plus icon and text | 100–150 ms; error card auto-expands |
| Approval requested | Pinned card with a steady accent frame, plus a notification if the tab is hidden | No looping attention-grabber; text states the exact action |
| Disconnect, failure, theme switch | Event-only glitch on a short label or frame | Under 300 ms, at most 2 slices, never on body text |
| First launch only | Boot sequence | 600–1200 ms, skippable on any key; 150 ms fade under reduced motion |

The guardrails are what keep this from becoming Cyberpunk 2077's HUD. The first is a set of **no-effect zones**: message bodies, markdown, code blocks, diffs, the composer, dense tables and long scrolling lists get static frames at most. Error *words* never glitch. Only the frame or icon around them may. The second is an **effect budget**: one ambient effect per view, interaction effects only on the hovered or focused element, and roughly three decorative animations at once at most. The third is **flash safety**. WCAG 2.3.1 forbids more than three flashes per second above the luminance thresholds, with a small-area allowance of about a 341×256 px rectangle at 1024×768, plus special rules for saturated red ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). So glitches should shift position or hue rather than toggle between dark and bright, and should never flicker saturated red. The fourth is **performance**: animate `transform` and `opacity`, keep blur and shadow static or on few elements, and add `will-change` only just before a change ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Continuous `clip-path` glitch animation forces repaints ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2017/12/21/css-glitch-effect/)). In practice, glow should live on a pseudo-element whose opacity animates, and conic `@property` borders should be limited to one or two live elements and paused when idle.

Sound is the most neglected liveness channel and the easiest to get wrong. Short synthesized Web Audio blips for send, tool start, success and error add a lot of presence. They should be muted by default, silent while the tab is hidden and on hover, and must resume the `AudioContext` only after a user gesture to comply with autoplay policy ([Chrome autoplay](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)).

## Build it as a theme layer with an effects dial, then measure it

The architecture that makes all of this maintainable is a theme layer, not a component framework. Generate semantic tokens in OKLCH/LCH from a few inputs, as Linear did. Base the palette on a blue-tinted near-black (around #0B0F14) with off-white text (around #D6DEE8), one dominant accent, one warning hue, and a contrast knob for a high-contrast variant. Ship the FUI decoration in a CSS cascade layer of pseudo-elements, so the same components render plain or FUI. For geometry, augmented-ui offers dependency-free, BSD-licensed clip-path chamfers, tabs and border/inlay layers through `--aug-*` properties. Its leading-comma custom properties trip some linters, and nested elements need resets ([augmented-ui docs](https://augmented-ui.com/docs/)), so a few hand-written `clip-path: polygon()` utilities are a reasonable alternative. GSAP is now **100% free including ScrambleText and SplitText** for commercial use, which makes it a viable orchestration engine if CSS and the View Transitions API aren't enough ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free)). The focus pattern that survives clipping, glow and forced colors is short enough to quote:

```css
.panel-wrap:focus-within { outline: 2px solid var(--accent); outline-offset: 3px; } /* unclipped wrapper */
@media (forced-colors: active) { .panel { border: 1px solid CanvasText; box-shadow: none; } }
@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce) { .holo { backdrop-filter: none; background: var(--panel); } }
```

User control comes next and isn't optional. Cyberpunk 2077 lets players hide each HUD area, which is a reminder that density and effect intensity are personal ([Super Jump](https://medium.com/super-jump/a-ux-analysis-of-cyberpunk-2077s-hud-f74afe6b9961)). Hermes should expose two settings. "Effects: Full / Subtle / Off" should default from `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-reduced-transparency`. The latter is not Baseline and works only as progressive enhancement, since MDN lists limited support ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency)). "Sound: Off / On" should default off. Reduced motion should still keep 100–150 ms opacity and color feedback, because state changes need acknowledgment even when decoration is gone. Add a compact/comfortable density toggle, keeping 24×24 px minimum targets as WCAG 2.2 AA requires ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Then ship the FUI theme the way Reddit and Skype learned to: opt-in, staged, with a plain theme always one click away.

Finally, decide the verdict with data. Because of the aesthetic-usability effect, satisfaction scores will flatter the theme. Track latency against the budget (≤100 ms direct manipulation, ≤400 ms navigation, determinate progress past 10 s), INP at the 75th percentile, frame drops while scrolling long sessions on a low-end machine, and task times for the core loop. That loop is to send a prompt, monitor the tool calls, approve one, stop and steer, and find a past session. A theme that lifts those numbers, or holds them while users choose to keep it on, has succeeded. One that only wins compliments has not.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "how much effect can a usable UI tolerate?" to "what is each effect reporting?" In film FUI and in productivity tools alike, motion that carries information is cheap in attention and makes the interface feel alive, while motion that carries nothing feels stiff even when it is technically smooth. That explains why a static cyberpunk copy feels dead. Its glow and scanlines are constant, so they signal nothing. It also explains why adding more ambient animation would make things worse. Hermes is unusually well suited to the alternative, because an agent app has a rich, real state stream (thinking, tool lifecycle, approvals, background cron, disconnects) that maps one-to-one onto the FUI vocabulary of frames, readouts and status lights.

Two uncertainties remain. No controlled study measures neon-glow legibility or the productivity cost of decorative motion in tools, so the specific alphas and durations here are informed practitioner judgment that Hermes should validate with its own task-time data. And if the chat surface remains the embedded xterm.js TUI, the most important productivity surface is largely out of CSS's reach. That makes a web-native step card and approval UI the highest-leverage investment, for usability as much as for style.
