# Train the eye, then make the machine enforce it

Good UI taste is a trained perceptual skill, not a gift. It comes from three things: studying excellent work actively (naming *why* it works rather than just liking it), rebuilding what you study, and producing enough of your own work to close the gap between what you can see and what you can make. Generic UI, whether made by people or by models, comes from the opposite: taking the highest-probability default when the brief is vague. Anthropic calls this **distributional convergence**, and it explains the familiar set of AI tells (Inter, indigo-to-purple gradients, centered hero plus three soft cards, emoji icons, fake round-number stats). It also explains a less obvious finding: banning one default just moves the model to the next one, so a static ban list goes stale. The fix that holds up is process, not taste-by-decree. That means a written brief and a concept taken from the product's world; references broken down into properties (preserve / borrow / reject) instead of screens to copy; several divergent directions instead of one polished guess; and a critique loop in which a *separate* evaluator looks at rendered screenshots against weighted criteria. Roughly half of "polish" can be measured from computed styles and screenshots: type sizes and line-heights, line length, spacing-grid adherence, APCA contrast, unique-value budgets, complexity and colorfulness. The rest (concept, coherence, brand fit, generic-ness) is better judged pairwise than scored. For OMNI/UI-MAX, the stat sheet should grade only what it can measure, and the skill should make the unmeasurable parts unavoidable. Concretely: add the missing craft and slop checks below, add a pairwise judge that compares against references and earlier iterations, and add a check that the rendered page actually matches what `DIRECTION.md` claims.

## Taste grows from articulated comparison, rebuilding and volume

The practitioners agree closely. Emil Kowalski (Sonner, Vaul; formerly Vercel and Linear) lists seven methods: surround yourself with great work, ask *why* you like something until gut reactions become explanations, study rather than consume, look past first impressions, create regularly, get feedback from skilled people, and expect early work to look weak. His most useful instruction is the negative one: **"don't label things as good or bad."** Name the reasons instead ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste)). Rauno Freiberg (Vercel) gives the most concrete exercises. Replay screen recordings in slow motion, because a gesture's start, travel and resolution can't be seen at full speed. Recreate interfaces in code to build intuition and vocabulary. Trace interactions back to physical analogs such as page turns, card throws and pinching. Treat your own daily frustrations as design prompts. He also notes that insight tends to arrive after making something and stepping away, not from "sitting down and just thinking hard" ([Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). His craft page works as both a public swipe file and a copywork log: dozens of small interaction experiments, each linked to a prototype ([rauno.me/craft](https://rauno.me/craft)).

The Ira Glass "gap" explains why this feels bad at first. Beginners have taste good enough to see that their work falls short, and the only cure is volume: "it's only by actually going through a volume of work that you're going to catch up and close that gap" ([rsms.me transcript](https://d.rsms.me/stuff/ira-glass-taste.txt)). One commentator points out that the gap can *widen* as taste improves, so it behaves like a moving horizon rather than a finish line ([Matt Rickard](https://blog.matt-rickard.com/p/the-taste-gap)). Karri Saarinen's rules for Linear add the organizational half. **"The best design is opinionated,"** made for a specific person rather than flexible for everyone. "The simplest way to increase quality is to reduce scope." Nothing ships below the bar. And "data can be a crutch," so Linear doesn't decide by A/B test and expects designers to trust a trained intuition ([Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/karri-saarinens-10-rules-for-crafting-products-that-stand-out/)).

The research supports the *active* form of study but has never tested a UI-specific training regimen. Art experts look at images differently from novices. They scan more widely and give more attention to background and composition ([Francuz et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00087/full)), and their aesthetic ratings don't drop as images become abstract, while laypeople's do ([Pihko et al. 2011](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2011.00094)). Perceptual-learning research distinguishes learning from mere exposure from *perceptual expertise*, which appears to need task-relevant training, as with a radiologist learning to read scans ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2025/entries/perceptual-learning/)). Both studies are small (about 20 people per group). The honest conclusion is that "study, don't consume" fits the evidence but has not been shown to improve UI judgment in a controlled study.

The strongest controlled result concerns iteration, not exposure. In Dow et al. (ACM TOCHI 2010), people who developed several web-ad designs **in parallel** outperformed those who refined one design serially. Their ads did better on click-through and expert ratings, raters judged them more diverse, and the participants gained more self-efficacy. Nearly half of serial-condition participants reacted negatively to critique, and no parallel participant did ([ProtoLab UCSD](https://protolab.ucsd.edu/?p=3561)). Replications are mixed: a physical-prototype follow-up was inconclusive, and Murphy et al. (2022) found clear parallel advantages in engineering design ([Murphy 2022](https://edrl.engr.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/142/2022/02/Murphy2022_Article_ComparingParallelAndIterativeP.pdf)). Taken together, these sources point to a regimen a developer can actually keep up:

| Drill | Cadence | What it trains | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swipe file with a written one-line *why* per item | On capture | Turning reactions into reasons | Kowalski |
| Recreate one admired component in code, then diff against the original | Weekly | Local detail, vocabulary | Rauno, Kowalski |
| Frame-step a screen recording of an admired interaction | Weekly | Timing, commit points, easing | Rauno |
| Degraded-variant comparison: break a good UI (extra colors, borders everywhere, off-grid spacing) and name why the original wins | Weekly | Task-relevant perceptual training | Synthesis from the expertise research; untested |
| Ship small public experiments | Continuous | Volume to close the gap | Glass, Rauno |
| Three divergent directions before converging | Per project | Better outcomes, less defensiveness | Dow et al. |

## References only help once you break them into properties

The reference landscape splits into four jobs, and the source type tells you how much to trust it. Libraries of **shipped product UI** (Mobbin, Refero, Page Flows, Nicelydone, SaaSFrame) show patterns that survived real users. Mobbin Pro costs $10/mo billed yearly and now includes an MCP for agents ([Mobbin pricing](https://mobbin.com/pricing.md)). Refero Pro includes an MCP and a "Refero Skill" for agents ([Refero docs](https://doc.refero.design/help/plans.md)). **Curated craft galleries** (Godly, Siteinspire, Awwwards Sites of the Day) carry an editor's filter. Godly shows entries as video, which captures motion that stills miss ([InspoAI](https://www.inspoai.io/blogs/godly-alternatives)). Awwwards picks fewer than 365 Sites of the Day from about 15,000 yearly submissions ([Lazyweb](https://design.lazyweb.com/best/ui-inspiration-sites)). **Like-driven communities** (Dribbble, Behance) are the weakest evidence of usability. Paul Adams' "Dribbblisation of Design" argued that shots optimize surface without problem context ([Yewknee summary](https://yewknee.com/blog/the-dribbblisation-of-design)), though defenders note that most shots are personal work, so a missing rationale is expected ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/in-defence-of-dribbble-and-artsy-uis-a0a2388994b6)). For game and FUI work, the free specialist archives are best. Game UI Database 2.0 holds **55,000+ screenshots from 1,341 titles**, filterable by HUD element ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/game-ui-database-20-is-the-ultimate-free-reference-tool-for-game-designers)), alongside HUDS+GUIS and Kit FUI ([HUDS+GUIS](https://www.hudsandguis.com/about)). One caveat: nearly every "best inspiration sites" roundup is written by a competing library, so treat the rankings as marketing.

Benchmark products need special care. Linear is now the cited standard for dark, dense power tools, but one 2026 analysis warns that the "Linear look" has become **the generic middle of SaaS design, and AI tools already default to it** ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)). Study Linear's *system*: three LCH inputs (base, accent, contrast) replacing 98 hand-set variables per theme ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Don't copy its surface.

How good designers use references is fairly well documented. NN/g's mood-board method starts from **4–5 mood words**, collects from varied and physical sources, gives key assets visual priority, and annotates each item with color-coded "take" and "avoid" notes. It warns that screenshot-heavy boards are really competitive boards, not mood boards ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/mood-boards)). Refero's published agent workflow is the clearest operational model. Separate Styles (visual language), Screens (structure) and Flows (the journey after the click). Open 3–4 strong matches, pick **one** primary direction, and borrow only specific details from the others. Write a **"reference lock"** stating what to preserve, borrow and reject, and keep a decision ledger that ties every choice to a source and a reason. Never copy one reference directly ([Refero skill workflow](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/referodesign/refero_skill/f78b4eccf112d7a179b92afeafdd7e8684560ac2/references/example-workflow.md)). Austin Kleon's good theft versus bad theft (steal from many, transform and credit, versus steal from one and imitate) supplies the ethics ([summary](https://medium.com/the-window-seat/steal-like-an-artist-10-things-nobody-told-you-about-being-creative-e73f2c016c85)). The law draws its line much later. GUI look-and-feel protection has narrowed since the early cases ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_and_feel)), and trade dress excludes functional elements, as in *Cub Club v. Apple*, where look-and-feel claims were rejected ([GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-3_21-cv-06948/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-3_21-cv-06948-0.pdf)). But reusing literal assets, code, icons or a distinctive brand's combination of marks is where the risk lies.

A practical test follows from all this. If you can name only one source for a screen, you are imitating. If you can name several, and the specific property taken from each ("type ratio from a Swiss poster, table density from Stripe, accent restraint from Vercel"), you are synthesizing. Cross-domain references (signage, instruments, print, film titles) are the cheapest route to distinctiveness because fewer people mine them, though no study measures this.

Feeding references to AI follows the same logic. **"'Clean and modern' is not a reference, it is an invitation to the average"** ([Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/meltedme_dev/articles/stop-asking-ai-to-make-it-modern)). Attach screenshots. Keep aesthetic references separate from composition references, because two images suggesting different layouts for the same screen confuse the model ([13labs](https://www.13labs.au/guides/ai-ui-that-doesnt-look-ai-generated)). Lock a small token file before building ([Dev.to](https://dev.to/dionis_uizze/stop-shipping-generic-ai-ui-why-ai-coding-tools-need-better-design-context-5ebn)). NN/g adds that naming a design system is fine, but asking AI to imitate a specific brand is not ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/vague-prototyping/)). A good reference packet therefore contains:

- a brief with mood words
- one primary style reference and at most two detail references, each annotated "take X, not Y"
- a composition reference that doesn't conflict with the style reference
- the extracted tokens
- a banned-defaults list
- one signature detail tied to the product's value

## Critique only works against stated goals, with alternatives on the table

Unstructured critique turns into a contest of preferences. NN/g's format makes it a test of whether the design meets objectives agreed in advance. Roles are presenter, critiquers and a **rotating** facilitator. Facilitators use round robin or quotas (two strengths, one improvement) to get people talking. Directive reactions get reframed as goal-linked questions, so "Yikes… that layout!" becomes "how does this layout help the user finish the task?". Problems aren't solved in the room. Teams can start with 30 minutes a week ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/)). For systematic coverage, run a heuristic evaluation in which 3–5 people work independently against Nielsen's heuristics, timeboxed to 1–2 hours, then cluster and prioritize together. A violation is not automatically a bug ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/)).

Fast perceptual checks can be added on top. The squint and grayscale tests from the Refactoring UI tradition check whether hierarchy survives blur and works without color ([playbooks.com summary](https://playbooks.com/skills/wondelai/skills/refactoring-ui)). Vercel's guidelines add checking at 50% zoom to simulate ultra-wide screens, and designing empty, sparse, dense and error states, not just the happy path ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). The Dow finding gives the structural rule: **bring 2–3 alternatives to critique**, because people absorb critique of one option among several without defending it. A lightweight version for a developer team: the author writes a three-line goal; everyone silently runs squint, grayscale, 50% zoom and state checks; a round robin follows; the author chooses the actions.

The same structure carries over to a machine critic. The one-design-at-a-time failure has an AI equivalent: Anthropic reports that **agents overrate their own work on subjective tasks**. That is why its long-running harness uses a separate, calibrated evaluator rather than self-review ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)).

## AI UI looks generic because ambiguity resolves to the mode, and bans just move it

The tells are consistent across independent sources, and they come in three layers. The **visual-default** layer is the most familiar. Anthropic names the unguided default as "Inter fonts, purple gradients on white backgrounds, and minimal animations" ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Jeff Humble's seven tells add aurora glows, emoji icons, cards on cards, multicolored side tabs and status dots on everything ([Fountain Institute](https://pages.thefountaininstitute.com/posts/7-tells-that-a-ui-is-ai-generated)). The **structural-template** layer covers composition. OpenAI tells GPT-5.4 to avoid a generic SaaS card grid as the first impression, dashboard-card mosaics, pill clusters, stat strips and decorative gradients standing in for the main visual idea ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/blog/designing-delightful-frontends-with-gpt-5-4)). The **content-and-UX** layer is the one that gets missed. It includes invented testimonials and round-number stats like "10,000+ brands," which are predictable enough to grep for ([DEV](https://dev.to/0x41414141/the-illusion-of-the-one-day-build-why-i-deleted-half-my-ai-generated-landing-page-5dfp)), and missing validation and error states ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). Fixing only the first layer still leaves a page that reads as machine-made.

The cause is statistical. Models sample the most probable design for an underspecified prompt, and the web corpus is saturated with Tailwind and shadcn tutorials and "safe" SaaS templates ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Adam Wathan's joking apology for making Tailwind UI buttons `bg-indigo-500` ("it needed to be something") is the folk origin of the purple ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). It is plausible, but no controlled study has tested it. Academic work confirms the outcome. "Interrogating Design Homogenization in Web Vibe Coding" argues that frictionless workflows amplify dominant conventions and proposes **"productive friction"**: deliberate prompts that make the creator question default output ([arXiv 2603.13036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13036)). Design Theater tested 5 generative UI tools on 120 interfaces. It found visual and layout convergence across tools, and found that **over 25% of the design rationales the tools stated to users were never implemented (34% for functional requirements)** ([arXiv 2607.22928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22928)). That second number matters for any skill. A model's description of its own design is not evidence.

The most important dynamic is second-order convergence. Anthropic found that banning Inter sent Claude to Space Grotesk ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Its current `frontend-design` skill now lists the **"anti-slop" looks models have since overused** as clusters to avoid unless the brief calls for them: warm cream with terracotta, near-black with a single neon accent, broadsheet hairline layouts with zero radius, and "template chrome" such as eyebrow labels, middle-dot meta strings, monospace data labels and arrows appended to links ([anthropics/skills](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). Over the same period the skill moved away from font lists and toward process:

- plan 4–6 named hex colors, the typefaces and their roles, and a layout concept
- review the plan against the brief and revise anything generic
- build and critique
- "spend your boldness in one place"

The UIX Ultra cyberpunk theme is a textbook case. It avoided purple and still read as "too vibe coded," because stock cyan and magenta, uppercase mono labels everywhere and uniform ornament are the cyberpunk version of the same mode.

The techniques with the best evidence attack the causes directly. In Anthropic's generator-evaluator harness, both agents share four criteria: design quality ("a coherent whole rather than a collection of parts"), **originality** (custom decisions versus "template layouts, library defaults, and AI-generated patterns"), craft and functionality. Quality and originality are weighted up, because Claude already handled craft and functionality acceptably. The evaluator drives the live page through Playwright, is calibrated with few-shot score breakdowns, and runs 5–15 iterations. Scores rose and then plateaued, and the author sometimes preferred a middle iteration. Even iteration 1 beat the no-prompt baseline. Letting the generator "refine or pivot" produced the most distinctive results, such as a Dutch museum site that jumped from a polished dark landing page to a spatial 3D gallery. One warning: criteria wording creates its own convergence. "Museum quality" pulled outputs toward a particular look ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). OpenAI's version is a hard composition test: **"if the first viewport could belong to another brand once the nav is removed, the branding is too weak."** It also says no cards by default, one job per section, and verify with Playwright across viewports ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/blog/designing-delightful-frontends-with-gpt-5-4)). PostHog adds the scaffolding needed for existing apps: give the model the token file, forbid values outside it, and write a one-sentence job-to-be-done "design compass" first ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/newsletter/vibe-designing)).

No controlled study isolates the effect of each technique. Preference arenas don't settle it either. UI-Bench's 4,000+ expert pairwise judgments put Orchids, Figma Make and Lovable above v0 and Replit ([UI-Bench](https://www.uibench.ai/leaderboard)), which suggests scaffolding matters as much as the base model. But arenas reward first-glance appeal and don't measure distinctiveness.

## Half of polish is measurable; the other half needs pairwise judgment

The fundamentals that can be checked from computed styles are well established. Butterick gives web body text of **15–25px**, line-height of **120–145%**, line length of **45–90 characters**, and **5–12% extra tracking** for all caps, which is acceptable for less than a line ([Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)). Utopia's fluid scales interpolate from about a **1.2 ratio on mobile to 1.333 on desktop** ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/blog/designing-with-fluid-type-scales/)). Refactoring UI, through summaries, prefers a hand-picked set (12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48…) and says hierarchy should come from weight and color before size ([Refactoring UI summary](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/claude-code-skills@0.5.0/data/skills/ux-ui-design-system/references/refactoring-ui.md)). Tailwind v4 derives every spacing utility from one `--spacing: 0.25rem` unit, so grid adherence reduces to a test of whether values are multiples of 4px ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwindcss-v4)).

Color should be handled in perceptual space. OKLCH keeps lightness consistent across hues, where HSL doesn't, but Evil Martians warns that "just OKLCH is not enough" and that contrast needs APCA ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)). APCA sets thresholds by use: **Lc 90** preferred for body text, **75** minimum for body columns, **60** for other content, **45** for large headlines, **30** for placeholder and disabled text, **15** for dividers and focus rings. Unlike WCAG 2's ratio, it doesn't overstate contrast near black, which matters for every dark theme ([APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)). Radix turns this into roles: a 12-step scale in which step 11 text is guaranteed Lc 60 and step 12 is guaranteed Lc 90 on step 2 ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)). That makes de-emphasis a token decision rather than a guess.

Counting unique values is an established signal of design debt, though nobody has published "good" thresholds. CSS-Tricks had 175 unique colors and 175 font sizes before its 2019 redesign ([Project Wallace](https://www.projectwallace.com/blog/the-css-tricks-redesign-in-numbers)), and GitLab had more than 70 font sizes ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/a-quick-css-audit-and-general-notes-about-design-systems/)). The useful heuristic is "30 font sizes when the design calls for 10" ([Tuts+](https://webdesign.tutsplus.com/understanding-css-stats-how-to-make-the-most-of-the-numbers--cms-22756t)). Project Wallace detects color aliases and can fail builds on user-set thresholds ([Project Wallace](https://projectwallace.com/blog/detecting-color-aliases)). The budgets below are inferred from design-system role counts (Material 3 has 15 type roles, iOS 11, Radix 12 steps). They are calibration targets, not norms, and should be measured on rendered pages after merging aliases:

| Property | Tight | Debt | Source of the number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct font sizes | ≤ 10 | > 20 | Inferred from M3 / iOS role counts; CSS Stats heuristic |
| Font families | ≤ 2 + 1 mono | > 3 | Inferred |
| Text colors (after aliasing) | 4–6 | > 10 | Radix two-level text roles |
| Border radii | ≤ 3–4 + pill | > 6 | Inferred |
| Shadow / elevation levels | ≤ 3–5 | > 6 | Inferred |
| z-index values | ≤ 6–8, no 9999 | 9999-style values | Inferred |
| Spacing values off the 4px grid | ≤ 10% | > 20% | Tailwind 4px base |
| Text below its APCA target | 0 | any body text | APCA table |

Screenshot-level metrics have older and better evidence than most people expect. Reinecke et al. (CHI 2013) rated 450 websites with 548 people. **Visual complexity (quadtree leaves, text groups, image areas) and colorfulness, plus demographics, explained 48% of the variance in 500 ms first-impression appeal**, and complexity mattered more than color ([Reinecke et al.](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke13aesthetics.pdf)). A follow-up with about 40,000 participants found that preferred levels vary by demographic group ([Reinecke & Gajos 2014](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke14visual.pdf)). So these metrics belong as percentiles against a reference corpus, not as pass/fail thresholds. Feature Congestion quantifies clutter from color, orientation and luminance contrast ([Rosenholtz et al.](https://typeset.io/pdf/feature-congestion-a-measure-of-display-clutter-1ed72kfuy1.pdf)).

Judgment-level evaluation is where VLMs fit, under constraints. UIClip, a CLIP model fine-tuned on about 2.3M UI screenshots, picked the preferred UI in a pair **75% of the time, against 53% for GPT-4V and 60% for Claude 3 Opus**. These figures come from a secondary summary ([UIClip summary](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2404.12500v1)). UICrit's 3,059 region-grounded designer critiques improved LLM feedback quality by 55% when used as few-shot examples ([arXiv 2407.08850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850)). Studies of VLM judges converge: **they rank more reliably than they score** ([arXiv 2604.25235](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25235v1)), they reach only about a third exact agreement on 5-point scales ([overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/vlm-as-a-judge)), and they under-penalize unreadable text and misalignment ([ImagenWorld](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27862)). This sets the division of labor. Deterministic probes own contrast, spacing, alignment and counts. The VLM judges concept, coherence and generic-ness, pairwise and region-grounded, and is never allowed to override a measured fact.

## OMNI/UI-MAX should grade what it can measure and force the rest into the loop

The repo already does much of this, which makes the gaps easier to see. `omni check` grades six stats (Access, Originality, States, Motion, Responsive, Craft), weights Originality highest at 1.3, gates on a11y, compositional slop and 390px overflow, and caps a failing gate at B. `src/slop.js` fires 15 tells, from tech gradients and default accent hue to ornament everywhere and halation. `src/craft.js` already measures APCA Lc by size and weight, all-caps tracking, paragraph line-height, and budgets for radii, shadows, z-indexes and weights. The skill runs brief → surface → direction → system → states → build → verify → deliver, with a taste log of the owner's verdicts. What's missing falls into four groups.

**First, close the measurable gaps in the stat sheet.** Each check below comes directly from the fundamentals above. It is cheap to compute from the existing capture, and it reports an actionable near-miss rather than a raw count.

| Add to | Check | Rule (from the sources above) |
|---|---|---|
| Craft | `type-scale-fit` | Cluster sizes; flag near-duplicates (13/14, 15/16 px) and sizes off a declared ratio |
| Craft | `display-tracking`, `heading-leading` | ≥ 32px with positive tracking, or heading line-height ≥ 1.5 → warn |
| Craft | `measure` per paragraph | Characters per line outside 45–90 (warn > 75) |
| Craft | `color-aliases`, `near-miss` | OKLCH ΔE < 0.02 pairs; radius 6 vs 8; #333 vs #343434 |
| Craft | `neutral-tint` | Mixed tinted/untinted grays or grays with different hues |
| Craft | `proximity` | Gap inside a group ≥ gap between groups, from bounding boxes |
| Craft | `alignment` | 3+ distinct left edges within 8px in a column |
| Craft | `emphasis-levels`, `competing-ctas` | > 3 text color levels; > 1 solid primary button per section |
| Originality | `gradient-text`, `nested-cards` | `background-clip:text` gradients; card inside card |
| Originality | `template-chrome` | Eyebrow labels, middle-dot meta, arrows on links, one accented headline word |
| Originality | `second-order clusters` | Cream + terracotta; near-black + one neon; hairline broadsheet at radius 0 |
| Originality | `fake-proof` copy grep | Round-number stats ("10,000+", "99%"), templated testimonials, placeholder copy |
| New "Visual" metrics | complexity, colorfulness, balance | Quadtree leaves, Hasler–Süsstrunk, mass symmetry, as percentiles vs a corpus |
| New "Visual" metrics | `squint-salience` | Blurred `squint.png`: does the top-salient region overlap the H1 or primary CTA? |

The slop list needs a version and a rotation policy. Second-order convergence means every tell added today will eventually be avoided by the model, and something else will take its place. Each tell should carry a "first seen" date and its source. Each benchmark run should also cluster the fonts, accent hues and layouts across runs, so that a new mode (say, every run choosing the same grotesk) shows up as a fresh tell. That is a measured kind of productive friction.

**Second, add a pairwise judge rather than a scored one.** An `omni judge a b` command should send two screenshots with UICrit-style questions to a separate critic model. The two screenshots could be the current iteration and the previous one, the build and its primary reference, or two variants. The critic should return a preference, region-grounded reasons tied to the rubric dimensions, and majority votes over several samples. It should never return a 1–10 aesthetic number. Its output feeds the critique step. Measured stats stay authoritative. The judge also enables Anthropic's two lessons: refine or pivot, and **keep the best iteration, not the last one**, which the skill's "max three iterations" rule currently cannot detect.

**Third, verify direction claims against the rendered page.** Design Theater's finding that a quarter of stated rationales go unimplemented points to a check no current tool performs. The skill already requires `DIRECTION.md`. If it declares tokens in a machine-readable block (accent hex and meaning, type families and ratio, ornament budget naming 2–3 selectors), `omni check --direction DIRECTION.md` can confirm several things: the accent appears and nothing else saturated dominates, the declared fonts actually load, ornament shows up only on the named elements, and the type scale fits the stated ratio. This converts "it has a point of view" from a promise into a partly checkable contract. It is also the first gate that rewards *intent*, not just the absence of tells.

**Fourth, make the skill train taste, not just apply rules.** The direction step should produce a Refero-style **reference lock**: one primary reference plus up to two detail references, each with what to preserve, borrow and reject, and a ledger tying each token to a source. References should come from shipped-product libraries for structure and from cross-domain archives (Game UI Database, Kit FUI, signage, instruments) for visual language. Dribbble and other web UIs of the same genre are excluded. Variants should differ in concept, as the skill already says, and should be critiqued side by side, per Dow. The critique prompt should add OpenAI's "remove the nav: could this belong to another brand?" test, plus the grayscale and 50% zoom passes. The taste log already captures verdicts. It should also capture the *degraded-variant* reason: when the owner rejects something, record which property caused it, so the Never list grows in properties (palette rule, label rule, ornament rule) and not only in artifacts. Finally, the benchmark should treat the human verdict as ground truth and track whether the stat sheet *predicts* it. The single B1 run so far scored a perfect 100 S with no verdict recorded yet. That is exactly the case the README warns about: a perfect script score means the script found nothing, not that the design is good.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. Taste looks like the scarce input, but for both people and models the scarce input is *specificity*: a concept from the product's world, references broken into named properties, and alternatives compared side by side. Generic output is simply what fills the space when specificity is missing, which is why banning the current defaults works for a while and then stops. A tool can't supply taste, but it can make the absence of specificity visible. The measurable half of polish should become non-negotiable and automatic. The unmeasurable half should be made explicit (a reference lock, a direction contract, pairwise verdicts) so that it can be checked, argued about and learned from.

Two cautions should shape the next iteration of OMNI/UI-MAX. Every proposed threshold here other than Butterick's, APCA's and the 4px grid is inferred from design-system role counts, not validated against a corpus of well-built sites. Calibrating them on a curated reference set is real work, not a formality. And a stat sheet that grows more complete can create its own convergence, just as "museum quality" did in Anthropic's harness. The more the score rewards one kind of tidy, the more every output will look like it. The guard is to keep the human verdict as the metric that matters and to watch whether the scores keep predicting it.
